---
title: Sign of the Rate of Change
description: If f is positive, the quantity function F is increasing; if f is negative, F is decreasing.

The rate-of-change function $f$ is really just reporting on its parent, the
[[Rate of change|quantity function]] $F$, where $f(x) = F'(x)$.

- $f(x) > 0$ (graph above the axis) $\Rightarrow$ $F$ is **increasing** there.
- $f(x) < 0$ (graph below the axis) $\Rightarrow$ $F$ is **decreasing** there.
- $f(x) = 0$ (graph crosses the axis) $\Rightarrow$ $F$ has a **relative max or min** there.

You never need to see $F$'s graph to know its shape — $f$'s sign already tells you.

**Math example:** for $f(x) = x - 2$: $f(x) > 0$ when $x > 2$, so $F$ is increasing
there; $f(x) < 0$ when $x < 2$, so $F$ is decreasing there.

**Real-world example:** if your bank account's rate of change is negative this month,
your balance is shrinking even before you check the total.
