---
title: Quantity Function F(x)
description: F(x) is the actual amount of something at input x — the thing being tracked, not its rate of change.
tags:
  - functions
---

I'm $F(x)$, the **quantity function**. I hold the actual value of something — a
distance traveled, a bank balance, a population size.

My best friend is $f(x)$, my [[Derivative]]: $f(x) = F'(x)$. Where I'm going up, $f$ is
positive. Where I'm going down, $f$ is negative. Together we tell the whole story: I say
*how much*, $f$ says *how fast I'm changing*.

**Math example:** $F(x) = 100 + 5x$ could be the dollars in a savings account after
$x$ months, so $F(0) = 100$ and $F(3) = 115$.

**Real-world example:** a car's total distance traveled since departure, a population
count, and a bank balance are all quantity functions — each one is a running "how much."
