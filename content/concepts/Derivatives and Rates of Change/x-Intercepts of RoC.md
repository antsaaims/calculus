---
title: x-Intercepts of the Rate of Change
description: Where the rate-of-change graph crosses the x-axis, the quantity function F has a relative max or min.
tags:

---

Where the RoC graph $f$ crosses the $x$-axis, $f(x) = 0$ — the quantity isn't changing
at that exact instant.

That crossing point is a **relative max or min** of $F$: $F$ was going one direction,
and after this point it goes the other way.

- $f$ crosses from **positive to negative** $\Rightarrow$ $F$ has a relative **max**.
- $f$ crosses from **negative to positive** $\Rightarrow$ $F$ has a relative **min**.

Compare this to [[Extrema of RoC]] — that one is about $f$'s own peaks and valleys, not
where $f$ crosses zero.

**Math example:** $f(x) = x - 3$ crosses zero at $x = 3$, going from negative to
positive, so $F$ has a relative min at $x = 3$.

**Real-world example:** the instant a car's velocity crosses from negative (backing up)
to positive (moving forward) is the moment its position is at a relative min.
