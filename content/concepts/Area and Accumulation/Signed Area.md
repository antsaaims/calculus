Hi, I'm signed area. I care about *where* you are, not just *how much* space there is.

If the region sits above the $x$-axis, I count it as **positive**. If it sits below, I
count it as **negative**. Add those pieces together (with their signs!) and you get me.

$$
\text{Signed Area} = \underbrace{(\text{area above axis})}_{>0} - \underbrace{(\text{area below axis})}_{>0}
$$

Signed area is what tells you the actual **change** in a quantity — because a negative
region means the quantity went *down* over that interval, not up.

**Math example:** if the area above the axis is $10$ and the area below is $4$, the
signed area is $10 - 4 = 6$.

**Real-world example:** a hiker's elevation-rate graph has uphill stretches (positive,
above the axis) and downhill stretches (negative, below). Signed area adds them up to
give the net change in elevation for the whole hike.
