---
title: Min and Max
description: A relative max is a local peak; a relative min is a local valley.
tags:
  - functions
---

A **relative maximum** is a point where the function is higher than everywhere
immediately around it — a local peak.

A **relative minimum** is a point where the function is lower than everywhere
immediately around it — a local valley.

See [[Recognizing a Max or Min]] for the increasing/decreasing pattern that gives one
away.

**Math example:** $f(x) = x^2$ has a relative (and absolute) minimum at $(0, 0)$ — every
nearby point sits higher.

**Real-world example:** a stock's price forms a relative max right before it dips —
the highest point in its immediate neighborhood, even if the price climbs even higher
weeks later.
