**Concave up** ($\cup$): the graph curves like the bottom of a bowl — it bends upward.

**Concave down** ($\cap$): the graph curves like a frown — it bends downward.

Concavity describes how a curve *bends*, which is a different question from whether it's
going up or down. A graph can be increasing and concave up, increasing and concave down,
or any other combination.

**Math example:** $f(x) = x^2$ is concave up everywhere; $f(x) = -x^2$ is concave down
everywhere.

**Real-world example:** money in a compound-interest account grows concave up — it
climbs faster and faster over time as interest earns interest.

Where a graph switches from one kind of bend to the other, see [[Extrema of RoC]] for
what that switch means for the underlying quantity.
