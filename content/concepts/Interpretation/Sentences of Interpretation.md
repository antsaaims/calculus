A calculus answer isn't finished until it's a sentence a non-math person could
understand. Every good interpretation sentence has four parts:

- **When** — what does the input value mean in context?
- **What** — the right subject/description for your sentence.
- **How** — the right action word: *"was ___,"* *"was increasing/decreasing by ___,"*
  or *"increased/decreased by ___."*
- **How much** — the value, with units.

**Describing a function:** "[$f(x)$ and its equation] [$f$ units] is the
[$f$ description], where [$x$] [$x$ units] is [$x$ description]."

**Point on the quantity function:** "When [input value + units + description],
[description] was [value] [units]."

**Area of a rate of change (change over an interval):** "From $a$ to $b$, [description]
[increased/decreased] by [area value] [units]."

**Point on the rate of change:** "At [input value + units + description],
[description] was [increasing/decreasing] by [value] [units] per [input unit]."

**Math example:** suppose $F(x)$ is gallons of water in a tank after $x$ minutes, and
$F(4) = 30$.

**Real-world example, filled in:** "At 4 minutes, the tank had 30 gallons of water."
That's the "point on the quantity function" template with **When** = 4 minutes,
**What** = the tank, **How** = "had," **How much** = 30 gallons.
